## 1. Invisalign — Best Overall (Editor's Choice)

**Average cost:** $3,000–$8,000  
**Treatment time:** 6–18 months  
**Doctor involvement:** High (in-office)  
**Best for:** Mild to severe cases, all ages

Invisalign has been the gold standard in clear aligner therapy since 1997. With over **14 million patients treated** and a network of more than 80,000 trained providers worldwide, it remains the most comprehensive and clinically validated option available.

### Why Invisalign Tops Our List

**SmartTrack® material:** Invisalign's proprietary aligner material applies more consistent force over time, improving predictability compared to generic aligner plastics used by competitors.

**Clincheck® software:** Before treatment begins, your orthodontist shows you a 3D digital simulation of your projected results — so you know exactly what to expect.

**Widest case range:** Invisalign treats everything from simple spacing issues to complex Class II/III malocclusions, severe crowding, and open bites that at-home brands simply cannot address.

**Attachments system:** Small tooth-colored bumps bonded to teeth help aligners exert precise forces on difficult-to-move teeth — a technique at-home brands typically skip.

### Invisalign Drawbacks

- Significantly more expensive than at-home alternatives
- Requires multiple in-office visits (typically every 6–8 weeks)
- Not available without a licensed orthodontist or dentist

### Invisalign Verdict

If your budget allows and you have any degree of moderate-to-complex alignment issues, Invisalign is the safest and most effective choice. For mild cases, the price premium may not be justified.

**Rating: 9.2/10**

## 2. Byte — Best for Speed & Mild Cases

**Average cost:** $1,895–$2,295  
**Treatment time:** 3–5 months average  
**Doctor involvement:** Remote (licensed dentist review)  
**Best for:** Mild crowding and spacing, adults

Byte has disrupted the at-home aligner market with its proprietary **HyperByte® vibration device**, which is included free with every treatment. This high-frequency vibration (HFV) technology is designed to accelerate tooth movement and improve aligner seating.

### Why Byte Is #2

**HyperByte® technology:** Clinical data suggests HFV can reduce treatment time by up to 50% compared to aligners worn without vibration devices — making Byte the fastest major aligner brand for mild cases.

**For-life guarantee:** Byte's Aligner Guarantee for Life provides free touch-up aligners after treatment if your teeth shift — one of the strongest warranties in the industry.

**All-day and at-night options:** Byte offers both a standard (22 hrs/day) and a nighttime-only (10 hrs/night) version. Byte-at-Night takes longer (5–6 months) but suits those who don't want to wear aligners during the day.

**Transparent pricing:** $1,895 (all-day) or $2,295 (nighttime) with no hidden fees. Financing available from $69/month.

### Byte Drawbacks

- Only suitable for mild to moderate alignment issues
- No in-person visits — requires self-managed impression kit at home
- Less appropriate for crowding requiring significant tooth movement

### Byte Verdict

For adults with mild crowding or spacing who want the fastest results at a fraction of Invisalign's price, Byte delivers exceptional value. Not suitable for complex cases.

**Rating: 8.7/10**

## 3. Candid — Best for Safety & Oversight

**Average cost:** $2,400–$3,200  
**Treatment time:** 6–12 months  
**Doctor involvement:** Medium-high (remote orthodontist)  
**Best for:** Mild to moderate cases, safety-conscious patients

Candid occupies the sweet spot between Invisalign's full in-office care and purely at-home brands. Every Candid patient is remotely monitored by a licensed orthodontist throughout treatment — not just at the beginning.

### Why Candid Is #3

**CandidMonitoring™:** Every Candid patient uses a proprietary scanning device (CandidSensor) to submit weekly progress scans. Your orthodontist reviews these and can flag issues early — a significant safety advantage over brands with no ongoing oversight.

**Licensed orthodontist oversight:** Unlike some competitors who use general dentists for reviews, Candid uses board-certified orthodontists exclusively.

**Refined case selection:** Candid only accepts patients whose cases they can effectively treat — meaning if you're not a good fit, they'll tell you rather than take your money.

**Studio network:** Candid has physical Studio locations in major U.S. cities for in-person scanning (no messy home impressions required).

### Candid Drawbacks

- More expensive than purely at-home brands
- Slower than Byte for mild cases
- Limited availability in some areas

### Candid Verdict

If you want the professional oversight of a specialist at a price significantly lower than Invisalign, Candid is the best-in-class choice.

**Rating: 8.5/10**

## Is At-Home Clear Aligner Treatment Safe?

At-home clear aligners are generally safe for **mild, uncomplicated cases** when patients are thoroughly screened. The primary risks of unsupervised treatment include:

- **Undetected underlying conditions** (gum disease, cavities) that can worsen during treatment
- **Root resorption** — minor shortening of tooth roots from excessive force
- **Poor fit** leading to bite issues if aligners aren't properly seated
- **Treatment failure** — teeth don't move as predicted

**The bottom line:** Have a professional dental checkup before starting any at-home treatment. This is non-negotiable for your long-term dental health.

## What to Ask Before Choosing a Brand

Before committing to any clear aligner brand, ask these questions:

1. **Am I a good candidate?** Most brands have online assessment tools. Use them — and supplement with a professional opinion.
2. **What's included in the price?** Does it cover refinements if treatment doesn't go as planned?
3. **What happens if my case changes?** Understand the mid-treatment adjustment policy.
4. **Who reviews my progress?** Know if you're being monitored by a dentist or orthodontist.
5. **What's the retainer situation?** Retention is lifelong — factor in ongoing costs.

## Clear Aligner Costs: What You'll Really Pay

The advertised price is rarely the full cost. Budget for:

- **Retainers:** $100–$500 per set; expect to replace them every 1–2 years
- **Dental X-rays and checkup:** $100–$300 if needed before starting
- **Refinements:** Additional aligners mid-treatment if results aren't perfect (free with some brands, $500–$1,000+ with others)
- **Financing interest:** At-home brands often partner with CareCredit or Affirm; always calculate total payback

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do clear aligners work as well as braces?**  
A: For mild to moderate cases, yes — studies show comparable outcomes. For severe misalignment, traditional braces or a combination approach may achieve better results.

**Q: Can I switch brands mid-treatment?**  
A: Technically yes, but it requires a new impression, new treatment plan, and additional cost. It's best to choose correctly from the start.

**Q: Are clear aligners covered by insurance?**  
A: Many dental insurance plans cover a portion of orthodontic treatment — often $1,000–$2,500 lifetime. At-home brands may not qualify. See our [insurance guide](/blog/does-insurance-cover-aligners) for details.

**Q: How important is wearing my aligners 22 hours a day?**  
A: Extremely. Compliance is the #1 predictor of treatment success. Wearing aligners less than prescribed leads to delayed or failed results.

**Q: What happens after I finish clear aligner treatment?**  
A: You'll need to wear retainers — initially full-time, then nightly for life — to maintain your results. This is non-negotiable.
